# iPhone gathering controls

Research date: 2026-09-30. This note combines current repository source with Apple’s HIG, API documentation, and first-party app guides. App guides establish documented workflows, not measured control dimensions or exact SF Symbol names. No UI was implemented or exercised on a device for this research.


Current decision (2026-10-01): selected items move through the Move menu. Stack dragging, fanning, and the drag destination tray were removed after device feedback. The comparisons below record the earlier research and do not describe the current interaction.

## Recommendation

Use a **lightweight gathering tray containing a compact draggable stack and a quiet native Move menu**. Put the count just above/outside the stack, replacing the separate “2 gathered” header. Keep tapping the stack to inspect its contents and put items back. Retain **Move to List + `folder`** for both individual and gathered snips: this is the existing iOS/Mac vocabulary and the previously approved menu direction. The gathered control should differ in target count, not action identity. ([iOS Move](../../SnipSnapiOS/MoveSnipMenu.swift), [Mac Move](../../SnipSnap/SnipListView.swift), [approved menu direction](../menu-cleanup-proposal.md))

This is our synthesis, not an Apple-prescribed tray. It retains the gather-and-drag interaction while borrowing the visible batch-action route documented in Files and Mail. Apple recommends a nondrag alternative to drag actions, and its menus guidance calls for consistent representations of common actions. ([Drag and drop](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/drag-and-drop), [Menus](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/menus))

## Documented precedents

| Source | Documented behavior | Implication for Snip Snap |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Files on iPhone** | Touch and hold one item for Move; for several items, More → Select → select items → an action such as Move at the bottom. ([Files guide](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/organize-files-and-folders-iphab82e0798/ios)) | The same move action can serve single and multiple targets; a bottom batch-action area is a first-party precedent. |
| **Mail on iPhone** | Select messages, tap the Folder button, then choose a mailbox. Its user guide places the mailbox action at the bottom of the message list. ([Mail support](https://support.apple.com/104971), [Mail guide](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/organize-email-in-mailboxes-iph376ef8aa3/ios)) | A folder-shaped control has a documented move-to-container association. These sources do not identify its exact SF Symbol. |
| **Photos on iPhone, iOS 27** | Select several items, then More → Add To → Album → choose an album. ([Current Photos guide](https://support.apple.com/en-ie/guide/iphone/iphee7aff030/ios)) | Selection followed by a contextual batch action is familiar. Album membership is **not** a move between exclusive lists; do not copy that action’s semantics. |
| **Apple selection guidance** | Lists should visibly communicate selection; SwiftUI `List` supports a selection set and, for touch multiselection in edit mode, checkmarked circles. ([Lists and tables](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/lists-and-tables), [SwiftUI List](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/swiftui/list)) | A native selection toolbar is a credible alternative if the product later favors in-place selected rows over a gathered bundle. |

## Three approaches and their tradeoffs

| Approach | Grounding | Tradeoff |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Compact multi-item drag preview with count** | Apple supports group dragging and grouped drag images. Its explicit numeric drag-badge guidance is in the **macOS** subsection. ([Drag and drop](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/drag-and-drop)) | Strongest expression of “these items travel together”; less space for readable content. A persistent iPhone stack and count are our adaptation, and need an explicit action route alongside dragging. |
| **Lightweight selection/action tray — recommended** | Files and Mail document bottom batch actions; a SwiftUI `Menu` presents destination actions using a text-and-symbol label. ([Files](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/organize-files-and-folders-iphab82e0798/ios), [Mail](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/organize-email-in-mailboxes-iph376ef8aa3/ios), [SwiftUI Menu](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/swiftui/menu)) | Preserves gathering and visible Move with less chrome. The small draggable stack within this tray is custom; its spacing and size still need visual verification. |
| **Native selection toolbar** | Apple’s iOS toolbar guidance prioritizes essential actions; native list selection supplies checkmarked rows. ([Toolbars](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/toolbars), [SwiftUI List](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/swiftui/list)) | Most conventional batch-action presentation, but a toolbar alone does not communicate the assembled draggable bundle. Favoring this direction would change the gathering experience beyond the current sizing/consistency feedback. |

## Concrete adjustments

**Shrink the resting preview’s footprint.** At research time the dock used full width, a minimum height of 88 points, 16-point internal padding, and a separate header/action row. A short snip therefore still received a large card. Let the preview size to a short content summary, with a maximum width for long text and room to grow at accessibility text sizes; keep only subtle backing layers. Preserve a 44 × 44 point hit region for the tappable stack too. Put the count above/outside its top edge, reserving space so it is neither clipped nor overlapping Move. The content sizing is a product recommendation from the reported blank-space problem, not an Apple dimension. ([Current dock](../../SnipSnapiOS/GatheredSnipsDock.swift), [Buttons](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/buttons))

**Make Move visually quieter while preserving a generous target.** At research time the label added horizontal padding and a 44-point minimum height inside `.glass`. Review the system style’s resulting footprint before layering more padding onto it. Prefer a compact system menu treatment over a prominent standalone glass pill. Apple’s buttons guidance gives **44 × 44 points as a general hit-region rule**; that need not be the visible fill’s size. Keep that activation area and separation from the preview even if the visible shape shrinks. `controlSize` changes system control size; `contentShape(.interaction, …)` controls hit testing and accessibility shape. Neither alone proves the final target is sufficient. ([Current dock](../../SnipSnapiOS/GatheredSnipsDock.swift), [Buttons](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/buttons), [controlSize](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/swiftui/view/controlsize(_:)), [Interaction shape](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/swiftui/contentshapekinds/interaction))

**Keep one label/icon pair.** Prefer the existing **Move to List + `folder`**. **Move… + `folder`** is a shorter alternative only if intentionally adopted consistently across matching entry points; it trades explicit destination wording for space. Apple recommends succinct action labels, consistent icons, and an ellipsis for menu items requiring further choices. That does not mandate punctuation on every menu-opening control. Avoid retaining `tray.and.arrow.down` for gathered Move alone: its different identity is unnecessary, and interpreting it as “receive into a tray” is our semantic reading, not an Apple prohibition. SF Symbols supports matching icon weight/scale to text; Apple does not mandate `folder` for this feature. ([Buttons](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/buttons), [Menus](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/menus), [SF Symbols](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/sf-symbols))

**Treat the count as selection state.** Show the actual nonempty gathered count next to the stack, including during dragging. Use a neutral/system-accent count chip with enough contrast, not a red unread-notification imitation. Apple’s notification-badge guidance is about app-icon unread counts and warns against mimicking that appearance; its macOS drag badge instead counts dragged items. Applying an attached count to a resting iPhone bundle is our inference. Expose “Gathered snips” with a value such as “2 snips,” and retain inspection and Move without dragging; do not make the number a separate unexplained accessibility control. ([Notifications](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/notifications), [Drag and drop](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/drag-and-drop), [SwiftUI accessibility modifiers](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/swiftui/view-accessibility))

## Limits and follow-up verification

No cited source establishes an ideal resting iPhone stack width/height, a mandatory Move symbol, or that a badge can replace explanatory text for every user. Exact dimensions should follow a real-app check, not be presented as HIG requirements. Verify one/many items, short/long text, long destination names, large Dynamic Type, VoiceOver, light/dark contrast, and reduced transparency. Confirm that the count remains readable above/outside the card, the smaller visible Move control retains its target, and dragging still exposes clear destination feedback. Apple asks for perceivable/adaptable controls and clear drop-destination feedback; this note does not claim those checks have passed. ([Accessibility](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/accessibility), [Drag and drop](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/drag-and-drop))
